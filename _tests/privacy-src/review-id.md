## Verdict

The translation is accurate and well judged. I found no change of meaning in any obligation. Dates, numbers and periods all match en.json: 15 Oct to 15 Nov 2026, 26 Oct to 26 Nov 2026, 28 Feb 2027, 31 Mar 2027, 30 days, 2 years, about one week, about one month, 12 months, five working days, past year and UEN. The obligation wording keeps its strength throughout: "hanya" (only), "kecuali jika hukum mewajibkan atau mengizinkan hal lain" (unless the law requires or permits otherwise), "mungkin"/"dapat" (may) and "akan" (will). Consent is consistently "persetujuan" and permission is consistently "izin", which matches the English split between PDPA consent and written media permission. The changes below are refinements; none corrects a serious error.

## Changes made

1. **Overseas transfer standard (s6, block 40).** Before: "tingkat perlindungan yang **setara** dengan". After: "**sebanding** dengan". Reason: "setara" means equivalent, which promises more than the PDPA "comparable" standard the English uses. "sebanding" is the faithful term.
2. **Retention table, published stories (s7, row 4).** Before: "hak Anda untuk **menarik diri**" (right to withdraw yourself / pull out). After: "hak Anda untuk **menarik kembali izin Anda**" (right to withdraw your permission). Reason: when a story is already published, "menarik diri" reads as quitting the campaign. The right that matters here is withdrawing the written media permission (see s3 Stage 2 and s11). This is interpretive, so please confirm (see residual notes).
3. **Retention table, working records (s7, row 5).** Before: "jika Anda **membatalkan pendaftaran**" (cancel your registration). After: "**membatalkan keikutsertaan**" (cancel your participation). Reason: s9 uses "membatalkan keikutsertaan" for "withdraw from a campaign". The two places now use the same term.
4. **Withdrawing consent and permission (s9 list item 4, s9 para "Withdrawing consent" heading and body, s11 para 1).** Before: "menarik persetujuan" / "menarik izin" / "Penarikan persetujuan". After: "menarik **kembali** persetujuan" / "menarik **kembali** izin" / "Penarikan **kembali** persetujuan". Reason: s3 and s11 already said "menarik kembali izin", so the wording was mixed. "menarik kembali persetujuan" is also the standard wording in Indonesian data-protection usage (UU PDP). It now appears 6 times, the same way each time.
5. **s11, events.** Before: "persetujuan yang **menjadi dasar kami**" (consent that is our basis; awkward). After: "persetujuan yang **kami jadikan dasar**" (consent we rely on). Reason: more natural Indonesian, same meaning.
6. **s9, media agreement (block 57).** Before: "perjanjian media terpisah yang telah Anda tandatangani". After: "... yang telah Anda tandatangani, **jika ada**". Reason: the English says "any separate media agreement". Without "jika ada" (if any), the text assumed every requester had signed one.
7. **Introduction, block 1.** Before: "Kampanye Share Your Story diselenggarakan" (number unmarked, so it could read as one campaign). After: "**Kedua** kampanye Share Your Story" (both campaigns). Reason: the English is plural, and there are two campaigns.
8. **s3 Stage 1, block 17.** Before: "kampanye yang Anda **daftari**". After: "kampanye **tempat Anda mendaftar**" (the campaign you registered for). Reason: "daftari" is a stiff, uncommon form for readers with modest literacy.
9. **s7, block 46.** Before: "untuk **sementara waktu**" (for a while). After: "untuk **waktu singkat**" (for a short time). Reason: matches "for a short time". "sementara waktu" leaves the length open.

## Residual notes (for a native-speaker check)

- **"lembar kerja (spreadsheet)" (s3, s6, s7, s8).** In Google Sheets terms, "lembar kerja" is one tab inside a file, while the English means one separate file per campaign. The gloss helps. A native speaker could check whether "spreadsheet" or "file spreadsheet" reads more clearly to PRT/PMI readers.
- **Change 2 ("menarik kembali izin Anda").** I narrowed the English "right to withdraw" to permission. If the drafters meant it to cover consent as well, use "menarik kembali izin atau persetujuan Anda".
- **s9, "apa akibatnya bagi Anda" (what the consequences are for you).** This is correct and fits the PDPA idea of consequences, but it is a little stronger than "what this means for you". "apa artinya bagi Anda" is a softer option.
- **s14, "Mandarin (aksara sederhana)".** This is understandable. "bahasa Mandarin (aksara sederhana)" or "Tionghoa Sederhana" are alternatives. I left the translator's choice, which is acceptable.
- **"akar rumput" for "ground-up" (block 1).** This is the natural equivalent. A reviewer could check whether readers might take it as political-activist wording.
- **"cookie", "browser", "di-hosting", "IP".** These loanwords are fine for this audience. A native speaker could confirm that "di-hosting" (block 39) reads naturally, or whether "disimpan/dihosting" is better.
